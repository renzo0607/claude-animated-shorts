# "The Tower Saved by a War" — original script v1 (2026-11-27)
Format: ~65 s vertical short, ~3.7 words/s (245 words), one steady narrator, our own characters and story.

## Facts (source: Wikipedia, "Leaning Tower of Pisa")
- Construction of the bell tower (campanile) of Pisa Cathedral began 9 August 1173.
- The tower began to lean in 1178, when construction had reached the second floor.
- Cause: a foundation only 3 m deep, set in weak, unstable subsoil (soft clay, sand, shells).
- Pisa was then almost continually at war with Genoa, Lucca and Florence; construction stopped for almost a century
  (resumed 1272, ~94 years later). That pause let the soil settle; otherwise the tower would almost certainly have toppled.
- To compensate, the builders made the upper floors taller on one side than the other, so the tower is curved.
- January 1990: closed to the public because of the lean (~5.5°).
- 1993-2001 stabilisation; soil was extracted from under the high (north) side; the tower was straightened by 45 cm
  (back to its 1838 position) and reopened in December 2001. Engineers deliberately kept the lean.

## Script
Italy's most famous building is only standing because a war stopped people from finishing it.
It's 1173, and the town of Pisa is rich, proud, and building the most beautiful bell tower in the world.
The builders finish the ground floor. Perfect.
They finish the first floor. Still perfect.
Halfway up the second floor, the foreman puts down his hammer and tilts his head.
The whole tower is leaning.
Its foundation is only three meters deep, sitting on soft wet clay.
And before anyone can fix it, Pisa goes to war, and the builders walk off the job for almost a hundred years.
When their grandkids finally come back, they have a genius plan: build the new floors taller on the short side.
Luckily, it didn't fall over.
Unluckily, now it's leaning and bent like a banana.
You see, the ground under Pisa is like an old mattress.
Ever sat on the edge of one? The side with your weight sinks, and the more it sinks, the more weight slides onto it.
That hundred-year war break gave the clay time to squeeze out its water and firm up.
Without it, the extra floors would have pushed the tower straight over.
By 1990 it was leaning so far that Italy closed it, and engineers spent eleven years sneaking dirt out from under the high side.
They straightened it by forty-five centimeters, and then stopped on purpose.
Because a tower that stands up straight is just a tower.
